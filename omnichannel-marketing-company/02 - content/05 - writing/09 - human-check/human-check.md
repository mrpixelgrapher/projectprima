# Human check — the operator's checklist

**Used by:** `00 - ENTRY.md` in this folder. **Origin:** the operator's instruction at the design lock (`00 - control/04 - source-intent/06 - operator-directives-2026-09-28-design-lock.md`): "making sure that it is written like a human showing high degree of clairty of thought compressing excessive knowledge into wisdom".

Read the piece aloud, once, straight through. Then answer each question. Any "no" is a FIX: quote the line and say what's wrong.

## 1. Written like a human

- [ ] Read aloud, does it sound like one person talking to another, and like the client in particular?
- [ ] Is it free of filler openers and closers ("In today's world…", "It is important to note…", "In conclusion…")?
- [ ] Do the sentences vary in length, the way people speak?
- [ ] Is every example specific (a name, a number, a place) rather than generic?

## 2. Clarity of thought

- [ ] Can you say what the piece argues in one sentence, after one read?
- [ ] Does each paragraph make one point, and does each point follow from the one before?
- [ ] Is there a moment where the piece faces the hard part (the tension) instead of stepping around it?
- [ ] Would a buyer who knows nothing about the topic follow it without re-reading?

## 3. Knowledge compressed into wisdom

- [ ] Does the piece leave the reader with something they can use or decide, not just facts they've been told?
- [ ] Is there a line worth remembering or repeating (the crafted surplus)?
- [ ] Has everything that doesn't change the reader's understanding been cut?
- [ ] Is the one thing something no single source says?

## 4. Nothing false

- [ ] Is every claim one you'd be comfortable having the client stand behind in front of their buyer?
- [ ] Is there no pressure the facts don't support (false urgency, invented scarcity)?

## 5. Native to the channel (variants)

- [ ] Would a regular user of the channel take it for something written there, not pasted from elsewhere?
- [ ] Does it end by sending the reader somewhere (the destination), in words that fit the channel?
