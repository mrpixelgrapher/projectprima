# 02 · Draft

**Purpose:** write the full article by expressing the spine: one movement per claim, every particular spent, every claim marked with its source. A well-argued, robotic draft is the correct output; voice comes next.

## Contract

- **Reads:** `25-writing/<P>/04-spine.md`, `03-observations.md`, `02-boundary-map.md` (sector 5); the piece brief (`24-planning/briefs/<P>.md`: format, keyword, links, path job, CTA, performance rules, image slots, limits); `draft-prompt.md` in this folder.
- **Writes:** `25-writing/<P>/05-draft.md`.
- **Done when:** every spine claim has a movement; every claim is marked `[src: O-n]` or `[src: L-nnn]`; the skeleton below is complete.

## Procedure

1. **Fill the prompt's slots.** `{{FORMAT}}` = the brief's long-form format and word range. `{{KEYWORD}}` = the confirmed keyword from the boundary map.
2. **Run the prompt.** Where it says `[src: observation-N]`, write `[src: O-N]`, or `[src: L-nnn]` for a single source.
3. **Keep to this skeleton:**
   - **Title:** contains the keyword; specific; says what the reader gets.
   - **Lead:** 2–3 sentences, opening on the reader's wrong model or on the tension. The keyword is in the first 100 words.
   - **Body:** one movement per spine claim, in spine order, with a subheading every 300–400 words.
   - **The turn:** where the tension is faced head-on.
   - **Close:** land THE ONE THING.
   - **Key takeaways:** 3–5 bullets.
   - **Call to action:** exactly the brief's CTA, pointing to the destination.
   - **Performance rules:** apply the brief's principles for this piece's path job, each only on its true basis (e.g. authority only where the proof is in hand).
4. **Link slots.** For each internal link the brief lists, insert `[LINK: <slug>]` where the argument touches that piece. The first piece of a topic has none.
5. **Graphic slots.** Insert at least 2 `[GRAPHIC: what it must show]` markers: one near the top (it becomes the featured image), and one at the most complex claim.
6. **Limits.** Nothing may be added that isn't on the spine, and nothing may breach the brief's Limits.

## Output template

    # <Title with keyword>
    <Lead>
    ## <Subheading>  … movements with [src: …] markers, [LINK: …], [GRAPHIC: …]
    ## Key takeaways
    ## <CTA heading>

## Worked example

Gemstone piece P2 `real-or-fake` *(illustrative)*, a lead: "Most HR teams assume a gemstone gift is real because the invoice says so [src: O3]. The one buyers remember is the one that showed them how to check it in under a minute [src: O5]."

## Self-check

- [ ] Does every spine claim have a movement, in order?
- [ ] Does every factual sentence carry a `[src: …]` marker?
- [ ] Is the keyword in the title and in the first 100 words?
- [ ] Are there ≥ 2 graphic slots, and a link slot for every link in the brief?
- [ ] Is the CTA exactly the brief's?

## Traps

- **Deciding while writing.** New claims that aren't on the spine. Cut them, or send the task back to research.
- **Generic paragraphs.** A paragraph with no particular is a hole. Spend sector 5.
