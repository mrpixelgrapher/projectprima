# 04 · Objectives

**Purpose:** say what success means in numbers the client agrees to, and how and when it will be read. `10 - delivery` records against these.

## Contract

- **Reads:** slot GOAL and slot LIMITS in `02-discovery/05-client-brief.md`; `03-strategy/03-channel-plan.md`.
- **Writes:** `03-strategy/04-objectives.md`.
- **Done when:** 1–3 objectives, each with a metric, baseline, target, source, reader and review date.

## Procedure

1. **Objectives.** Write 1–3, taken from GOAL. Each names **one** metric the client can see: enquiries, orders, meetings booked, subscribers, replies.
2. **Baseline.** Use the client's current number [client]. If they don't know it, the first objective is *"establish the baseline"*: measure for the first 30 days, and set the target after that.
3. **Target.** Use only a number the client stated or approves. Never invent one. An unapproved target becomes an approval item in `05 - approval/`.
4. **Reading.** Name where the number is read (inbox, Substack dashboard, order book), who reads it, and when (a date 30 or 90 days after the first publication).
5. **Tie to channels.** Each objective names the channels in the plan expected to move it.

## Output template

    # Objectives — <task id>
    | # | Objective | Metric | Baseline | Target | Read where / by whom | Review date | Channels |

## Worked example

Gemstone task *(illustrative)*: O1: "first corporate orders" · metric: enquiries from HR leads · baseline: 0 (a new line) · target: 10 qualified enquiries in 60 days (client approves) · read where: the enquiry inbox, by the founder · review: 60 days after launch · channels: LinkedIn, email, sales collateral.

## Self-check

- [ ] Does every objective have a single, visible metric?
- [ ] Does every baseline and target come from the client, or is it marked as an approval item?
- [ ] Is there a review date?

## Traps

- **Vanity metrics.** Impressions are not a business objective unless the client's GOAL is awareness.
- **Invented targets.** A number the client never agreed to becomes a broken promise at delivery.
