# 13 — Blog as Knowledge Production

Type: workflow redesign (Gap 03)

## The Gap

Blog creation currently collapses knowledge acquisition and drafting into a single phase. This produces shallow outputs because drafting occurs before sufficient knowledge exists.

## Redesigned Workflow

### Four Distinct Stages

```
Stage 1: Knowledge Acquisition (open-ended, accumulative)
    → Topic becomes a knowledge domain
    → Sources, frameworks, observations accumulated
    → Exit: Knowledge Sufficiency threshold met

Stage 2: Contextual Synthesis (pattern discovery)
    → Identify relationships between sources
    → Discover contradictions, second-order implications
    → Form original observations
    → Exit: Original insights documented

Stage 3: Insight Formation (selective, reductive)
    → Choose the single core argument for this article
    → Determine supporting evidence structure
    → Outline narrative arc
    → Exit: Article structure defined

Stage 4: Drafting (the shortest stage)
    → Write from accumulated material
    → Synthesis, not discovery
    → Quality review and publication
    → Exit: Published article
```

### Key Insight

Drafting should be the SHORTEST and EASIEST stage because most of the intellectual work is already done. If drafting feels hard, an earlier stage was skipped.

### CE Workflow Changes

| Current Behavior | Required Change |
|---|---|
| Topic → search → draft | Topic → research domain → synthesis → insight → draft |
| Article as final output | Article as visible layer; research artifacts preserved |
| Research as transient intermediate | Research as permanent, reusable asset |
| Drafting as discovery | Drafting as synthesis only |
| Content calendar drives production | Research saturation drives production |

### Anti-Pattern Detection

CE should detect and reject these patterns in blog workflows:
- **"Write an article about X"** → No research domain exists yet. Start with Stage 1.
- **"Search the web and summarize"** → This skips Stages 2 and 3. No original insight possible.
- **"Here's an outline, fill it in"** → The outline may be correct but Stage 1 was never done.
- **"Write 5 articles this week"** → Production velocity without knowledge accumulation.

### Content Planning vs. Content Creation

These are separate activities and must never be conflated:
- **Content planning:** Topic selection, audience mapping, publication calendar. Does NOT produce articles.
- **Content creation:** The four-stage workflow above. Produces articles.

A content calendar does not create meaningful articles. It creates pressure to publish before knowledge exists.
