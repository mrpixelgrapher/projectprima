# 01 · Positioning

**Purpose:** state, in one sentence, why the buyer should choose this client over the alternatives. Every pillar, piece and variant argues from that sentence, so it may only claim what the client can prove.

## Contract

- **Reads:** `02-discovery/05-client-brief.md`; `CONTEXT.md`.
- **Writes:** `03-strategy/01-positioning.md`.
- **Done when:** the problem, alternatives, difference and position statement are written with citations. For F1 = NEW or TO STRATEGY, 3–5 name options and one recommendation are also written.

## Procedure

1. **Problem.** Write the primary buyer's problem in their own words: one paragraph built from the brief's "fears", "wants" and "words they use" rows, with citations.
2. **Alternatives.** List what that buyer does today instead, from the market map.
3. **Difference.** List what the client offers that the alternatives leave unmet. Keep a difference only if the brief's Proof section backs it with a claim that is *in hand*. Move differences that are only asserted to "Claims to prove", which become approval items. Cut any difference that has neither.
4. **Position statement.** Fill in exactly: *For <buyer> who <problem>, <brand> is the <category> that <difference>, because <proof>.* Use one sentence and no adjectives the proof doesn't carry.
5. **Brand name** (only when F1 = NEW or TO STRATEGY). Give 3–5 options. For each: what it means, how it relates to the parent brand, and whether the domain or handle is available [VERIFY: check the registrar and platforms]. Recommend one, with a one-line reason tied to the buyer. The client decides at `05 - approval/`.
6. **Third party** (only when F1 = THIRD-PARTY). The position belongs to the third party. Build it from their materials, supplied by our client, and cite them.

## Output template

    # Positioning — <task id>
    ## Problem (in the buyer's words)
    ## Alternatives
    | Alternative | What it leaves unmet | Evidence |
    ## Difference
    | Difference | Proof in hand | Evidence |
    Claims to prove: <asserted-only differences, or "none">
    ## Position statement
    For … who …, … is the … that …, because ….
    ## Brand name (NEW / TO STRATEGY only)
    | Option | Meaning | Relation to parent | Available? | |
    Recommendation: … — because …

## Worked example

Gemstone task *(illustrative)*: "For HR leads at 50–300-person IT and consulting firms who need festival and client gifts that don't look generic, Kora Gifts is the corporate gifting line that delivers certified-gemstone desk pieces on a festival deadline, because every stone ships with its lab report and orders are fixed 30 days ahead." Name options: Kora Gifts, Stoneline Corporate, … Recommended: Kora Gifts, because it is short, reads as a gift, and keeps the parent's name.

## Self-check

- [ ] Does the problem use the buyer's words, with citations?
- [ ] Does each difference have proof in hand?
- [ ] Does the position statement follow the template exactly?
- [ ] For NEW: are there 3–5 names, a recommendation and a reason?

## Traps

- **A position the client wishes were true.** No proof in hand, no difference.
- **A category too broad to choose.** "Gifts" competes with everything; "corporate gemstone gifting" can be chosen.
- **Writing for the client's taste.** The statement serves the buyer's problem, not the founder's pride.
